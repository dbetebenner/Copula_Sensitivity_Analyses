    ``Precision'' is not one thing. It depends on what is being resampled.

    \vspace{4pt}
    \textcolor{pairedTeal}{\bfseries Paired subsampling.}
    Sample the \emph{same} student indices at both time points: draw
    $(U_i, V_i)$ pairs by resampling indices $\{i_1, \ldots, i_n\}$ from
    the subgroup. This preserves the latent covariance
    $\mathrm{Cov}(U_i, V_i)$ and typically yields \emph{tighter}
    repeated-sampling precision.

    \vspace{4pt}
    \textcolor{indepRed}{\bfseries Independent cross-sectional resampling.}
    Sample grade-$t_1$ and grade-$t_2$ populations independently: different
    students contribute to $F_U$ and $F_V$. This matches the
    NAEP/TIMSS use case and produces \emph{wider} uncertainty because no
    within-student covariance anchors the bridge.

    \vspace{4pt}
    Without distinguishing these two worlds, readers will conflate:

    \vspace{2pt}
    \hspace{5pt}$\bullet$\;linked-subsample stability (optimistic),

    \vspace{1pt}
    \hspace{5pt}$\bullet$\;unlinked-cross-sectional operating precision
              (realistic), and

    \vspace{1pt}
    \hspace{5pt}$\bullet$\;bridge accuracy (a separate axis).

    \vspace{4pt}
    Those are three different beasts. Panel~C makes the design dependence
    visible so that the precision curves in Panel~D can be read correctly.$^{\mbox{\footnotesize{3}}}$
