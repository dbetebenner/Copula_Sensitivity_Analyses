$^{3}$ Other regime families considered include the truncated exponential family
(the maximum-entropy distribution on a finite interval with a fixed mean) and the
truncated uniform family. If acceptable accuracy and precision are already achieved
under the Beta family, additional growth-regime optimization and fine-tuning should
only improve the recovered fit.

\vspace{4pt}
$^{4}$ Here, $U$ denotes the subgroup’s prior-grade reference quantile (pseudo-observation), 
and $C_0$ is the canonical baseline copula selected from STEP 1 by content area and year span. 
The fitted growth regime $\widehat{H}_S$ (distribution of latent $P=\SGPc/100$) and $C_0$ 
define a forward map \[ (U,\;C_0,\;\widehat{H}_S)\ \longmapsto\ F_{\widehat{H}_S}(v)=\mathbb{E}_U\!\left[\widehat{H}_S\!\left(F_0(v\mid U)\right)\right], \] 
with corresponding induced density $f_{\widehat{H}_S}(v)$. The latent SGPc distribution itself 
is given by $\widehat{H}_S$ (density $\widehat{h}_S(p)=d\widehat{H}_S/dp$), which is estimated 
by matching $F_{\widehat{H}_S}$ to the observed current marginal.

\vspace{4pt}
$^{5}$ We assume $P\perp U$. That is, $p$ is drawn independently of starting
point within the subgroup; meaning there is no correlation between the starting point $u$ and
the growth regime. This assumption is investigated more deeply in the full paper.

\vspace{4pt}
$^{6}$ The Beta family, $\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha, \beta)$, is reparameterized via
$\alpha = \kappa m$ and $\beta = \kappa(1-m)$, so that $m = \mathbb{E}[P] = \alpha / (\alpha + \beta)$
(the regime center, mean \SGPc~on the 0--1 scale) and $\kappa = \alpha + \beta$
(the concentration parameter).

\vspace{4pt}
$^{7}$ Alternative minimum-distance criteria (e.g., Cramer--von Mises,
$\int_0^1 \{F(v)-G(v)\}^2\,dv$) are also valid for regime fitting. If acceptable
accuracy and precision are already achieved under Wasserstein--1, then additional
growth-regime optimization and fine-tuning should only improve the recovered fit.}
