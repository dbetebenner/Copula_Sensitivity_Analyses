    Suppose longitudinal linkage is available: each student $i$ in subgroup $S$
    has a prior quantile $U_i$ \emph{and} a current quantile $V_i$. With these
    linked pairs, we can compute the true latent conditional percentiles
    \[
      P_i \;=\; F_0(V_i \mid U_i),
    \]
    where $F_0$ is the conditional CDF from the baseline copula $C_0$. The
    \emph{truth benchmark} for the subgroup is
    \[
      \thetaTrue \;=\; 100\,\mathbb{E}(P_S).
    \]

    \vspace{4pt}
    This is the quantity we would report if individual-level linkage existed.
    Panel~A shows the density $f_{H_S}(p)$ of the true growth regime alongside
    the bridge-recovered density $f_{\HShat}(p)$. The vertical lines mark their
    respective means.

    \vspace{4pt}
    The fundamental role of Panel~A is to \emph{define what accuracy means}:
    accuracy can only be assessed relative to a well-defined truth. Without
    a linked-data benchmark, ``accuracy'' becomes internal consistency rather
    than genuine error measurement.$^{\mbox{\footnotesize{1}}}$

    \vspace{4pt}
    The small displacement between the amber (true) and black (bridge)
    densities is the \emph{bridge cost}: the price paid for erasing
    individual-level linkage and relying on the copula bridge instead.
