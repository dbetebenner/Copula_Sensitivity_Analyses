    Precision quantifies the repeated-sampling spread of the bridge estimator
    $\thetaHat$ as a function of subgroup size $N$. Panel~D shows the 95\%
    confidence interval width under both resampling designs from Panel~C:
    \[
      \text{Precision} \;=\; \text{CI width}_{95}(\thetaHat)
      \;=\; 3.92 \cdot \mathrm{SE}(\thetaHat).
    \]

    \vspace{4pt}
    Two curves appear:

    \vspace{2pt}
    \hspace{5pt}$\bullet$\;\textcolor{pairedTeal}{\bfseries Paired} (solid):
    the optimistic bound from bootstrap subsampling with linked indices.
    At $N = 500$: CI width $\approx \ciWidthPairedFiveH$ SGPc points.

    \vspace{1pt}
    \hspace{5pt}$\bullet$\;\textcolor{indepRed}{\bfseries Independent} (dashed):
    the operational bound matching NAEP/TIMSS-style cross-sectional designs.
    At $N = 500$: CI width $\approx \ciWidthIndepFiveH$ SGPc points.

    \vspace{4pt}
    Precision improves sharply from small $N$ into the mid-range (200--800),
    then flattens. The amber dotted line at 5~SGPc points marks a
    practical threshold: below this width, subgroup mean \SGPc\ becomes
    operationally useful for reporting and comparison.

    \vspace{4pt}
    The gap between the two curves is the \emph{design effect}: the
    additional uncertainty from not having linked cohorts. This gap is
    largest at small $N$ and shrinks (absolutely) as $N$ grows, but the
    \emph{ratio} remains roughly constant.$^{\mbox{\footnotesize{4}}}$
