\small\raggedright
\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}

\fnline{1}{In the synthetic validation setting, the true growth regime is
$P_S \sim \mathrm{Beta}(\trueAlpha, \trueBeta)$ with mean $\SGPc = \trueRegimeMean$
and $\kappa = \trueKappa$, under a baseline $t$-copula
($\rho = \copulaRho$, $\mathrm{df} = \copulaDf$).
The bridge-recovered estimate uses the same copula family and
$\mathrm{Beta}$ regime class, evaluated by Wasserstein-1 minimum distance.}

\vspace{4pt}
\fnline{2}{Bridge accuracy here refers to the displacement introduced solely
by erasing individual-level linkage and relying on marginal-only information
to estimate the growth regime. It does \emph{not} include sampling variability
(that is precision, Panel~D) or model-choice sensitivity (stress tests, Panel~E).}

\vspace{4pt}
\fnline{3}{The paired-vs-independent distinction matters operationally because
most large-scale assessment programs (NAEP, TIMSS, PIRLS) use independently
drawn cross-sectional samples. Precision estimates based on paired subsampling
of a longitudinal cohort are systematically tighter than what users of the
method will experience in the field.}

\vspace{4pt}
\fnline{4}{The precision curves assume the baseline copula and regime family
are correctly specified. Model misspecification introduces additional uncertainty
not captured by these curves; see the stress-test component in Panel~E.
At large $N$ ($> 2{,}000$), bridge accuracy (Panel~B) typically dominates
sampling precision as the primary error source.}

\vspace{4pt}
\fnline{5}{Category stability is assessed by asking: would the subgroup's
classification change under perturbation of the estimate by one standard error,
by the bridge bias, or by the stress-test envelope? Robust classifications
survive all three perturbations. Fragile classifications require either larger
samples, tighter bridge accuracy, or wider reporting bands.}

\vspace{6pt}
\fnline{6}{\textbf{Model-choice stress tests} (right margin of Panel~E band):
copula family sensitivity (Frank vs.\ $t$), relaxation of the $P \perp U$
assumption, and classification changes under perturbation. These are
second-order uncertainty components that bound the ``unknown unknowns''
of the copula bridge.}
