\footnotesize\raggedright
\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}

\fnline{1}{The transform $x \mapsto u = \Fxref(x)$ is invertible when $\Fxref$
is continuous (strictly increasing on its support). In discrete or
mixed-continuous settings, the CDF is not strictly monotone and the
generalized inverse $F^{-1}(u) = \inf\{x : F(x) \ge u\}$ is used.
This distinction matters when working with score scales that have mass
points (e.g., integer-valued test scores). In such cases, randomized
ranks or mid-rank adjustments are sometimes applied to ensure a
continuous marginal on $[0,1]$; see Genest \& Ne\v{s}lehov\'{a} (2007)
and R\"{u}schendorf (2009) for details on pseudo-observation
construction under ties.}

\vspace{4pt}
\fnline{2}{The reference CDFs $\Fxref$ and $\Fyref$ are computed over the
\emph{full assessment population}---the complete set of examinees
across all subgroups in a given grade and year. They are \emph{not}
subgroup-specific. This ensures that all subgroups are mapped to
the \emph{same} reference scale, so that a quantile of $u = 0.30$
means ``at the 30th percentile of the population'' regardless of
which subgroup the student belongs to. The subgroup's marginal
density of $U$ then reflects its distributional position within
the population. In the margin strips, the grey $U(0,1)$ band is
the population baseline; subgroup density and the conditional
leaver/entrant proportion overlays are interpreted relative to it.}

\vspace{4pt}
\fnline{3}{Stayers are students observed in both the prior and current
assessment rounds; after PIT mapping, their linked pairs $(u_i, v_i)$
provide paired draws governed by copula $C$. Leavers appear only
in the prior round (prior-only observations) and entrants appear only
in the current round (current-only observations). All three groups
contribute to the marginal distributions, but only stayers contribute
to the empirical copula.}

\vspace{4pt}
\fnline{4}{In cross-sectional assessment programs (NAEP, TIMSS),
independent samples are drawn at each occasion and no student-level
pairing exists. The analyst observes only the marginal distributions,
so the copula $C$ must be supplied externally---typically from
canonical copula tables calibrated on longitudinal datasets in
similar content areas and year-spans (see the Overview infographic,
Panel~B1). The non-linkage premium calculations in the companion
appendix quantify the precision cost of this information loss.}

\end{minipage}
