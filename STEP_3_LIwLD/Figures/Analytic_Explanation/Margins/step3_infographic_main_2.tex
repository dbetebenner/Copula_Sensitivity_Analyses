\footnotesize\raggedright
\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}

\fnline{1}{The scatter plot depicts individual pseudo-observation pairs
$(u_i, v_i)$---a density-level view of the data showing where mass
is concentrated. Sklar's theorem, however, operates at the level of
\emph{distribution functions}, not densities. The CDF contour lines
overlaid on the scatter convert the density picture into an
equivalent distributional one: each contour traces the locus
$\{(u,v) : C(u,v) = c\}$, showing how much probability mass is
accumulated below any point in $[0,1]^2$. Reading the two views
together---scatter for shape, contours for accumulation---is
essential for understanding the factorization that Sklar's theorem
provides.}

\vspace{4pt}
\fnline{2}{Sklar's theorem (1959) guarantees that every bivariate
distribution function $H$ with continuous marginals admits a
\emph{unique} copula representation $H(x,y) = C(F_X(x), F_Y(y))$.
The copula $C$ captures all dependence information that is invariant
to monotone transformations of the margins.  The CDF strips on the
bottom and left of the figure visualize the marginal transforms
$F_X^{\mathrm{ref}}$ and $F_Y^{\mathrm{ref}}$; the copula $C$
governs the interior pattern.}

\vspace{4pt}
\fnline{3}{A level curve $\{(u,v) : C(u,v) = c\}$ is the locus of all
pseudo-observation pairs for which exactly a fraction $c$ of the
population has both prior and current scores below the
corresponding quantiles.  Under the independence copula
$\Pi(u,v) = uv$ these curves are rectangular hyperbolas; positive
dependence ``pulls'' them toward the diagonal.  The $C = 0.5$
contour partitions the unit square into two equal-probability
regions.}

\vspace{4pt}
\fnline{4}{The empirical Bernstein copula is a smoothed non-parametric
estimator constructed from the linked stayer pairs.  The parametric
\textit{t}-copula, fitted by maximum pseudo-likelihood, provides a
parsimonious summary with explicit tail dependence parameter
$\lambda$ and degrees-of-freedom~$\nu$. Close agreement between
the two sets of contours indicates that the parametric model
captures the essential dependence features of the data.}

\vspace{4pt}
\fnline{5}{The conditional CDF $F_{V|U}(v \mid u) = \partial C(u,v) /
\partial u$ yields a student-level growth percentile for each
linked pair $(u_i, v_i)$.  Fixing $u$ and tracing through the
contour levels effectively reads off the quantile function of $V$
given~$U = u$. Sensitivity of these growth percentiles to copula
choice can be assessed by comparing the conditional distributions
implied by the empirical and parametric copulas.}

\end{minipage}
